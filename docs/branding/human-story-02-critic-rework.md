# Human Story — Independent Critic + REWORK

Issue: #69
Reviewed: `human-story-01-definition.md`

## Verdict on V2

**REWORK**

V2 is substantially better than a generic developer bio, but it still explains Sebastián too much through engineering abstractions. It is warm in intent, yet several phrases could still belong to a polished professional profile rather than a recognizable person.

## Main findings

### F1 — Start with the non-linear path, not the location
`Vivo en Mendoza, cerca de la montaña` is true, but not the most compelling first sentence. The more distinctive human fact is that Sebastián did not arrive at software through a single straight professional track.

### F2 — Reduce abstract engineering nouns
Long sequences such as `actores, dependencias, excepciones, información, responsabilidad` are accurate but shift the section back toward Method/Capabilities. Human Story needs more plain language.

### F3 — Do not turn the mountain into a productivity metaphor
The V2 ending over-explains mountain as observation/adaptation/decision-making. That risks sounding manufactured. Mountain can simply be something Sebastián genuinely enjoys and part of the place he chose to live.

### F4 — Make motivation more concrete
The strongest motivation is not “systems thinking” as an abstract trait. It is wanting to understand a real problem well enough to build something genuinely useful and reliable around it.

### F5 — Preserve contradiction and range
Part of the personality is moving comfortably between different worlds: enterprise systems and product development, technical operations and software, technology and mountain life, Spanish and new languages. That range is more memorable than another list of competencies.

## Revised narrative strategy

Use a three-part arc:

1. **Where I came from:** a non-linear technical path.
2. **What that taught me:** software is used by real people under non-ideal conditions.
3. **What keeps me moving:** curiosity, learning and life outside the screen.

## Recommended public copy — V3

### Eyebrow
**Un poco sobre mí**

### Headline
**No llegué al software por un camino recto. Y eso terminó siendo una ventaja.**

### Body

> Soy Sebastián Ojeda. Vivo en Uspallata, Mendoza, y antes de concentrarme en construir productos pasé por mundos bastante distintos: soporte técnico, infraestructura, SAP e integraciones, operaciones y desarrollo de software.
>
> Ese recorrido cambió mi manera de programar. Me acostumbró a mirar más allá de la interfaz y a preguntarme qué está intentando resolver una persona, qué información necesita, qué pasa cuando algo falla y qué tiene que seguir funcionando incluso cuando el escenario deja de ser ideal. Por eso me atraen especialmente los proyectos que nacen de un problema concreto y terminan convirtiéndose en una herramienta que alguien realmente puede usar.
>
> También hay una parte de mí que simplemente disfruta aprender. Puedo pasar de profundizar una arquitectura o explorar datos y SAP a practicar un idioma nuevo porque me gusta entender cosas que todavía no conozco. No me interesa acumular tecnologías por sí mismas; me interesa ampliar mi capacidad para entrar en un problema nuevo y comprenderlo mejor.
>
> Y cuando no estoy frente a una pantalla, la montaña ocupa un lugar importante en mi vida. Vivir en Uspallata no es sólo una ubicación en el pie de página: es parte de mi día a día y de la clase de mundo real que me interesa mantener cerca mientras construyo tecnología.

## English compact version

### Eyebrow
**A little about me**

### Headline
**My path into software was not a straight line. That became an advantage.**

> I’m Sebastián Ojeda, a software developer based in Uspallata, Mendoza. My background crosses technical support, infrastructure, SAP and integrations, operations, and software development, so I learned early that a system is much more than its interface.
>
> I like to understand the problem before choosing the technology: what the person using it needs, where the process breaks, and what still has to work when the situation is not ideal. Those are the problems I most enjoy turning into software.
>
> I am naturally curious and usually learning something, from architecture, data or SAP to languages and unfamiliar domains. Away from the screen, the mountains are an important part of my life and of why I chose to live in Uspallata.

## Why V3 is stronger

- Starts with a real biographical shape rather than a professional slogan.
- Uses concrete parts of Sebastián’s path without becoming a CV chronology.
- Explains why the portfolio contains operational systems rather than simply asserting a positioning.
- Mentions learning and languages as personality, not as credentials.
- Keeps mountain/Uspallata authentic and literal rather than turning it into a forced engineering metaphor.
- Gives a recruiter a memorable human detail while preserving the professional narrative.

## Recommended BUILD direction

Use the **compact V3** on the homepage. Keep the longer V3 as source material for a future dedicated About page if the portfolio later needs one.

No portrait should be introduced until Sebastián explicitly approves a real image.

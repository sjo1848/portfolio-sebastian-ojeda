# Human Story — Definition

Issue: #69
Status: **DEFINITION / REWORKED**

## Objective

Add enough personal narrative to make the portfolio feel human and memorable without turning it into a biography or weakening recruiter scanability.

The section must answer a different question from Projects, Capabilities and Experience:

> **What shaped Sebastián’s way of working, and what keeps him interested in building software?**

## Audience effect

After reading this section, a recruiter or technical lead should not merely know that Sebastián is a Full-Stack developer. They should understand that:

- he arrived at software through real technical and operational contexts, not only through UI work;
- he is motivated by understanding how a system behaves in practice and where a workflow breaks;
- he values useful, verifiable software over decorative demos;
- he has a persistent learning drive across software, data, SAP/integrations and languages;
- Mendoza, mountain/field context and outdoor life are part of the person, but not a professional costume.

## Narrative pillars

### 1. Curiosity that moves toward real systems
Sebastián is not primarily motivated by frameworks or novelty. The recurring pattern is wanting to understand how something actually works: actors, dependencies, exceptions, information flow, responsibility and failure modes.

### 2. A non-linear technical path as an advantage
His background crosses software development, enterprise systems/integrations and hands-on operational environments. The point is not to enumerate jobs; it is to explain why he naturally thinks beyond the screen.

### 3. Building things that survive contact with reality
The portfolio already shows a preference for product behavior, traceability, QA, deployment and evidence. The human story should explain that this is not merely methodology; it reflects what he finds satisfying about engineering.

### 4. Learning as a constant, not a slogan
His interest spans software engineering, data, SAP/BTP/integrations and languages. This should be framed as sustained curiosity and willingness to enter unfamiliar domains, not as a list of courses.

### 5. Place and mountain as a human signal
Living in Mendoza/Uspallata and having an affinity with mountain/trekking provides a real personal anchor. It must remain personal rather than become mining/outdoor branding.

## Tone

The section should sound like a person explaining how they became the engineer shown in the rest of the site.

It should be:
- first person;
- concrete;
- reflective but not sentimental;
- warm without being casual;
- modest about claims;
- specific enough that it could not belong to any random developer.

Avoid:
- “apasionado por la tecnología”;
- generic resilience / challenge language;
- motivational autobiography;
- full employment chronology;
- personal hardship;
- hobby lists with no connection to personality.

## Recommended placement

**After Selected Work and before Capabilities.**

Narrative order:
1. Hero — what I build.
2. Selected Work — proof.
3. Human Story — who is behind the work and what shaped that approach.
4. Capabilities — what I can execute.
5. Experience — professional context.
6. Method — how I structure delivery.
7. Contact.

## Recommended section identity

Eyebrow: **Un poco sobre mí**

Headline: **No llegué al software por un camino recto. Y eso terminó siendo una ventaja.**

## Recommended public copy

The current recommended public copy is the compact V3 defined and critiqued in `human-story-02-critic-rework.md`. It supersedes the earlier V2 draft.

## Visual design hypothesis

The section should not be another card grid.

Preferred composition:
- warm-paper or stone field, not another dark technical panel;
- one large editorial headline;
- 2–3 short paragraphs in a generous reading column;
- a side rail with three quiet personal signals: `Uspallata · Mendoza`, `Montaña`, `Aprendizaje continuo`;
- no icons required;
- no fake portrait/avatar;
- a real portrait may be explored only if Sebastián explicitly supplies/approves one later.

The section should lower the visual tempo after Projects. Its job is emotional recognition, not another density peak.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Feels specific to Sebastián rather than reusable portfolio copy.
2. Adds warmth without weakening the Full-Stack professional identity.
3. Does not expose sensitive/private information.
4. Does not turn mountain/Mendoza into a professional niche signal.
5. Does not duplicate the Experience section line by line.
6. Reads naturally in ES and EN.
7. Adds no unsupported claim.
8. Homepage reading time stays reasonable.
9. Projects remain the dominant proof surface.
10. Human story changes how the reader interprets the existing portfolio instead of merely adding hobbies.

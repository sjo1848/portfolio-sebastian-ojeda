# Human Story — Definition

Issue: #69
Status: **DEFINITION / candidate PASS**

## Objective

Add enough personal narrative to make the portfolio feel human and memorable without turning it into a biography or weakening recruiter scanability.

The section must answer a different question from Projects, Capabilities and Experience:

> **What shaped Sebastián’s way of working, and what keeps him interested in building software?**

## Audience effect

After reading this section, a recruiter or technical lead should not merely know that Sebastián is a Full-Stack developer. They should understand that:

- he arrived at software through real technical and operational contexts, not only through UI work;
- he is motivated by understanding how a system behaves in practice and where a workflow breaks;
- he values useful, verifiable software over decorative demos;
- he has a persistent learning drive across software, data, SAP/integrations and languages;
- Mendoza, mountain/field context and outdoor life are part of the person, but not a professional costume.

## Narrative pillars

### 1. Curiosity that moves toward real systems
Sebastián is not primarily motivated by frameworks or novelty. The recurring pattern is wanting to understand how something actually works: actors, dependencies, exceptions, information flow, responsibility and failure modes.

### 2. A non-linear technical path as an advantage
His background crosses software development, enterprise systems/integrations and hands-on operational environments. The point is not to enumerate jobs; it is to explain why he naturally thinks beyond the screen.

### 3. Building things that survive contact with reality
The portfolio already shows a preference for product behavior, traceability, QA, deployment and evidence. The human story should explain that this is not merely methodology; it reflects what he finds satisfying about engineering.

### 4. Learning as a constant, not a slogan
His interest spans software engineering, data, SAP/BTP/integrations and languages. This should be framed as sustained curiosity and willingness to enter unfamiliar domains, not as a list of courses.

### 5. Place and mountain as a human signal
Living in Mendoza/Uspallata and having an affinity with mountain/trekking provides a real personal anchor. It can communicate patience, autonomy, adaptation and preference for tangible environments, but it must remain personal rather than become mining/outdoor branding.

## Tone

The section should sound like a person explaining how they became the engineer shown in the rest of the site.

It should be:
- first person;
- concrete;
- reflective but not sentimental;
- warm without being casual;
- modest about claims;
- specific enough that it could not belong to any random developer.

Avoid:
- “apasionado por la tecnología”;
- “desde chico me encantan las computadoras” unless factually documented and intentionally chosen;
- generic resilience / challenge language;
- motivational autobiography;
- full employment chronology;
- personal hardship;
- hobby lists with no connection to personality.

## Recommended placement

**After Selected Work and before Capabilities.**

Narrative order:

1. Hero — what I build.
2. Selected Work — proof.
3. Human Story — who is behind the work and what shaped that approach.
4. Capabilities — what I can execute.
5. Experience — professional context.
6. Method — how I structure delivery.
7. Contact.

This placement is intentional: the visitor first gets professional proof, then enough personal context to reinterpret the rest of the portfolio through a human lens.

## Recommended section identity

Eyebrow: **Más allá del código**

Headline candidate: **Me interesa entender cómo funcionan las cosas de verdad.**

Alternative headline: **El software es una parte del sistema.**

Recommended: the first. It sounds more human and less like another engineering slogan.

## Recommended Spanish copy — V2

> Soy Sebastián. Vivo en Mendoza, cerca de la montaña, y llegué al desarrollo de software por un camino bastante menos lineal que el de sentarme a aprender un framework y quedarme ahí.
>
> Pasé por sistemas empresariales, integraciones, soporte técnico y trabajos ligados a operaciones reales. Esa mezcla terminó definiendo lo que más me interesa de la ingeniería: entender quién usa un sistema, qué información necesita, dónde aparecen las excepciones y qué pasa cuando algo falla fuera del escenario ideal.
>
> Por eso disfruto más construir una solución que ordena un proceso real que una demo pensada sólo para verse bien. Me atraen los problemas donde hay que conectar backend, interfaces, datos, personas y reglas hasta que el sistema empieza a tener sentido como un todo.
>
> También soy una persona muy curiosa. Suelo estar aprendiendo algo: profundizando software y arquitectura, explorando datos y SAP, practicando idiomas o tratando de entender un dominio nuevo. No porque quiera acumular tecnologías, sino porque me gusta ampliar la cantidad de problemas que puedo comprender y resolver mejor.
>
> Fuera de una pantalla, la montaña es uno de los lugares donde más cómodo me siento. Probablemente por la misma razón que me atrae construir sistemas: obliga a observar, adaptarse, tomar decisiones con información incompleta y avanzar con criterio. Esa combinación entre curiosidad técnica, mundo real y ganas de seguir aprendiendo es la parte más personal de cómo trabajo.

## Recommended English copy — V2

> I’m Sebastián. I live in Mendoza, close to the mountains, and my path into software has been less linear than simply learning a framework and staying there.
>
> I have worked around enterprise systems, integrations, technical support and real operational environments. That mix shaped what interests me most about engineering: understanding who uses a system, what information they need, where exceptions appear, and what happens when something fails outside the ideal scenario.
>
> That is why I enjoy building a solution that organizes a real process more than a demo designed only to look good. I am drawn to problems where backend services, interfaces, data, people and rules have to connect until the system makes sense as a whole.
>
> I am also deeply curious. I am usually learning something: going deeper into software and architecture, exploring data and SAP, practicing languages, or trying to understand a new domain. Not to collect technologies, but to expand the range of problems I can understand and solve well.
>
> Away from a screen, the mountains are one of the places where I feel most at home. Probably for the same reason I enjoy building systems: they require observation, adaptation, decisions with incomplete information, and steady judgment. That mix of technical curiosity, real-world context and continuous learning is the most personal part of how I work.

> Soy Sebastián, desarrollador de software radicado en Mendoza. Mi camino combina desarrollo, sistemas empresariales, integraciones y experiencia cerca de operaciones reales; por eso suelo mirar un producto como algo más amplio que su interfaz.
>
> Me interesa entender quién usa un sistema, cómo circula la información, dónde aparecen las excepciones y qué ocurre cuando las cosas dejan de seguir el camino ideal. Disfruto especialmente los problemas en los que backend, datos, interfaces, personas y reglas tienen que funcionar como un todo.
>

Esa mezcla entre tecnología, mundo real y aprendizaje continuo es probablemente lo que más define mi manera de trabajar.

## Visual design hypothesis

The section should not be another card grid.

Preferred composition:
- warm-paper or stone field, not another dark technical panel;
- one large editorial headline;
- 2–3 short paragraphs in a generous reading column;
- a side rail with three quiet personal signals: `Mendoza`, `Montaña`, `Aprendizaje continuo`;
- no icons required;
- no fake portrait/avatar;
- a real portrait may be explored only if Sebastián explicitly supplies/approves one later.

The section should lower the visual tempo after Projects. Its job is emotional recognition, not another density peak.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Feels specific to Sebastián rather than reusable portfolio copy.
2. Adds warmth without weakening the Full-Stack professional identity.
3. Does not expose sensitive/private information.
4. Does not turn mountain/Mendoza into a professional niche signal.
5. Does not duplicate the Experience section line by line.
6. Reads naturally in ES and EN.
7. Adds no unsupported claim.
8. Homepage reading time stays reasonable; compact variant available.
9. Projects remain the dominant proof surface.
10. Human story changes how the reader interprets the existing portfolio instead of merely adding hobbies.

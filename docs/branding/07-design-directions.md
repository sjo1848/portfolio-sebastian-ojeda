# Direction A — Systems Ledger

## Idea
A warm, editorial technical system that presents the portfolio like a carefully authored record of systems built, decisions made and evidence available.

It should feel **precise, operational and mature**, without looking like a terminal, blueprint or imitation of another developer’s “plate/index” system.

## First-screen composition

**Role label**
`FULL-STACK SOFTWARE DEVELOPER`

**Primary headline candidate**
`Del proceso al sistema.`

**Supporting proposition**
`Construyo software end-to-end para flujos reales: backend, interfaces, datos, integraciones y automatización con evidencia verificable.`

English candidate:
`From workflow to system.`

Role remains explicit above the headline and in document metadata.

## Visual language

### Palette
- Canvas / warm stone: `#F4F1EA`
- Paper / surface: `#FFFDFC`
- Ink: `#111820`
- Muted graphite: `#59636D`
- Copper signal: `#A94F1C`
- Copper dark: `#843A13`

This intentionally evolves the palette already present rather than replacing it.

### Typography direction
- strong neutral sans for identity and body;
- restrained mono for indices, project state, evidence metadata and method markers;
- no decorative display font required.

Design test candidates: Inter/Geist-like sans + robust system mono fallback. Font choice must pass performance/licensing review before BUILD.

### Signature motif
**System traces**: thin segmented rules connecting small nodes/labels, used sparingly at section boundaries or project metadata.

Not circuit boards. Not flowchart wallpaper. The motif should imply connected systems and traceability.

### Project treatment
- one flagship case with evidence and narrative side-by-side;
- three selected cases below with consistent screenshot frames;
- evidence signals reduced to one concise proof line rather than multiple competing pills;
- stack limited on cards; deeper detail remains in case studies;
- project numbers remain navigational metadata, not a branded “plate” vocabulary.

### Interaction
- subtle border/translation changes on hover;
- no large parallax or cursor gimmicks;
- reduced-motion produces a complete, equally branded experience.

## Strengths
- strongest alignment with systems/operations positioning;
- preserves current stone/ink/copper investment;
- distinctive without becoming niche;
- works with screenshots and case-study depth;
- low risk of recruiter confusion.

## Risks
- can become overly procedural if evidence/index labels are repeated too often;
- requires disciplined whitespace and typography to avoid “documentation site” feeling.

# Direction B — Operational Atlas

## Idea
A more human/editorial identity inspired by field operations, routes and the Mendoza/Cuyo context. It connects software with real places and real workflows without presenting Sebastián as an outdoors or mining brand.

## First-screen composition

**Role label**
`FULL-STACK SOFTWARE DEVELOPER`

**Headline candidate**
`Software que funciona más allá de la pantalla.`

**Supporting proposition**
`Conecto procesos, actores, datos e interfaces para convertir operaciones reales en sistemas claros y confiables.`

English candidate:
`Software that works beyond the screen.`

## Visual language

### Palette
- Sand: `#F2EEE5`
- Paper: `#FFFDF8`
- Deep slate: `#182126`
- Copper: `#A65124`
- Muted sage/field signal: `#667469`

### Typography direction
- editorial display serif or humanist sans for large headings;
- neutral sans body;
- mono used minimally for technical proof.

### Signature motif
**Abstract route/field lines**: one-line paths, coordinates or contour-like fragments appearing only at major transitions and social-card/background details.

No mountain silhouettes, mining symbols or repeated topo-map wallpaper.

### Project treatment
Projects feel like “field cases”: larger project photography/screenshots, shorter metadata, a clear problem → system → proof rhythm.

## Strengths
- most personal/regional direction;
- visually memorable;
- reinforces “real-world operations” strongly;
- could make the site feel less like a conventional developer portfolio.

## Risks
- highest chance of being interpreted as outdoor/mining branding;
- a serif + route motif may compete with technical recruiter expectations;
- requires careful restraint to avoid visual metaphor overwhelming evidence.

# Direction C — Product Proof

## Idea
A highly polished, screenshot-led portfolio where the visual brand comes from typography, spacing, product frames and evidence—not a visible motif.

This is the most recruiter-efficient and lowest-risk direction.

## First-screen composition

**Role label**
`FULL-STACK SOFTWARE DEVELOPER`

**Headline candidate**
`Construyo productos y sistemas end-to-end.`

**Supporting proposition**
`Backend, interfaces, datos, integraciones y automatización aplicados a problemas concretos.`

English candidate:
`I build end-to-end products and systems.`

## Visual language

### Palette
- Near-white: `#FAF9F6`
- Surface: `#FFFFFF`
- Ink: `#14191E`
- Muted: `#626B74`
- Copper action: `#A94F1C`

### Typography direction
- one strong sans family with aggressive hierarchy;
- mono only where technically meaningful;
- large, clean project titles and restrained body copy.

### Signature motif
None beyond a highly consistent **evidence frame** and thin rule system.

### Project treatment
- screenshots dominate;
- project card copy is shortened substantially;
- one visual proof signal;
- one primary CTA;
- project pages carry technical depth.

## Strengths
- clearest recruiter scan;
- strongest product screenshots;
- easiest to implement and validate;
- lowest performance/interaction risk.

## Risks
- less memorable than A or B;
- can converge toward a premium but generic product-designer/developer template;
- relies heavily on every featured project having strong visual evidence.

## Design team recommendation

**Direction A — Systems Ledger** is the strongest standalone direction.

Recommended constraint: borrow **C’s screenshot-first restraint** inside A. In other words, the identity comes from typography, spacing, copper signal and subtle system traces; project evidence still dominates over branded decoration.

Direction B remains a useful exploration but is not recommended as the base because the regional metaphor introduces unnecessary classification risk.

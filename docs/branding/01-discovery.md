## 1. Hypothesis

The portfolio already communicates technical breadth, case studies and evidence, but it does not yet create a sufficiently memorable personal brand. The branding increment must improve recognition and coherence **without reducing recruiter readability, inventing credibility signals or turning the site into a decorative showcase**.

The working question is not “how do we make it prettier?” but:

> What should a recruiter, client or technical peer understand and remember about Sebastián Ojeda after the first 8–10 seconds?

## 2. Canonical constraints

- The primary public brand is **Sebastián Ojeda**.
- The current recruiter-facing positioning as **Full-Stack Software Developer** is evidence-backed and must not be replaced casually.
- Operational software, backend, applied AI, automation, integrations and real-world workflows are differentiators, not separate competing identities.
- FALDEO may remain a laboratory/company concept, but it must not displace the personal brand in this portfolio.
- No fabricated metrics, clients, production claims, screenshots, awards or seniority.
- ES/EN, accessibility, responsive behavior, reduced-motion support and performance are release requirements.
- No BUILD before DEFINITION + DESIGN + Human Gate.

## 5. What the references consistently do well

### 5.1 They answer “who are you?” before “what tools do you know?”
The best examples do not begin with a cloud of technologies. Role and proposition come first; stack supports the claim later.

### 5.2 Projects are treated as products or case studies
A strong project card is a doorway into a story: problem, responsibility, implementation, evidence and result/state. This matches the portfolio’s current direction and should be strengthened rather than replaced.

### 5.3 The strongest portfolios have a visual grammar
Myles is the clearest example: repeated naming, numbering, palette, page structure and editorial devices create identity. The lesson is **consistency**, not copying the drafting aesthetic.

### 5.4 Evidence beats self-description
Live status, screenshots, role, architecture, GitHub, accessibility notes, metrics where defensible and detailed case studies are more persuasive than adjectives such as “passionate”, “innovative” or “expert”.

### 5.5 Progressive disclosure works
The homepage should scan quickly. Deep technical content belongs in project detail pages. Sebastián already has case-study routes, so branding should improve the transition between scan and depth rather than adding more homepage density.

## 6. Current portfolio audit

### 6.1 Strengths to preserve

- **Clear job-market category:** `Full-Stack Software Developer · Backend, IA y Automatización`.
- **End-to-end proposition:** backend, interfaces, data, integrations, infrastructure and applied AI are already connected in the copy.
- **Curated project hierarchy:** four primary cases plus two complementary cases.
- **Real evidence:** screenshots, case pages, repositories and explicit limits.
- **A real method:** IDEA → DISCOVERY → DEFINITION → DESIGN → BUILD → VALIDATE → RELEASE → LEARN is differentiating evidence of process.
- **Bilingual architecture:** ES/EN is already first-class rather than an afterthought.
- **Accessibility/performance foundations:** semantic structure, focus handling, reduced-motion support and release QA are already part of the implementation.
- **Promising palette foundation:** warm canvas, dark ink/night and copper-like accent already avoid the default blue-SaaS look.

### 6.2 Brand weaknesses

#### A. The hero identifies the role but not the memorable idea
`Full-Stack Software Developer · Backend, IA y Automatización` is useful for recruiters but reads like a role taxonomy. The supporting copy explains end-to-end work, yet there is no short proposition that someone is likely to remember later.

#### B. Typography is functional but generic
The current stack relies on Inter/system sans with monospace accents. It is readable, but it does not yet create a distinctive editorial voice.

#### C. The palette exists as UI tokens, not yet as a brand system
Canvas, surface, night and copper accent work together, but there are no documented roles such as primary brand field, evidence surface, signal color, muted field, illustration rule or contrast rule.

#### D. There is no signature visual motif
Cards, pills, grids and gradients are competent UI patterns, but none is uniquely associated with Sebastián. The method, operational context and Cuyo/mountain background provide possible source material, but any motif must remain subtle and non-literal.

#### E. Project cards risk becoming metadata-heavy
Role, evidence chips, stack chips, status and two CTAs improve verification, but excessive badges can make the portfolio feel like an admin dashboard rather than a crafted professional narrative.

#### F. The method has high differentiation but can visually compete with the work
Project Method is a real asset; however, the recruiter’s first goal is to understand what Sebastián builds. The method should reinforce the project stories, not become a second product competing for attention.

#### G. Minor language inconsistency exists
The Spanish project section currently uses the eyebrow `Selected work`. If intentional bilingual editorial language is not defined as a brand rule, this reads as inconsistency rather than identity.

#### H. Personal presence is weak
The current site is deliberately proof-driven, which is good, but almost all personality is encoded indirectly through systems and projects. A personal brand needs one or two recurring human signals without becoming biography-heavy.

## 7. Tensions that Definition must resolve

### T1 — Recruiter keyword vs memorable proposition
Keep “Full-Stack Software Developer” highly visible for search/recruiter comprehension, while adding a short value proposition that is uniquely Sebastián.

### T2 — Software generalist vs operational specialization
The evidence supports hospitality, rentals, logistics, commerce, SAP/integrations, backend and applied AI. Branding should unify these under a systems/operations lens without narrowing the candidate pool too much.

### T3 — Personal brand vs FALDEO
Sebastián is the hiring identity. FALDEO may appear as a lab/studio context later, but it should not create ambiguity about who owns the work.

### T4 — Technical credibility vs visual elegance
Evidence, role and stack matter, but the homepage must breathe. The brand system should reduce repeated metadata visually rather than adding more decorative content.

### T5 — Distinctiveness vs imitation
The reference portfolios prove that a named visual system and repeated motifs work. The resulting system must be derived from Sebastián’s own evidence and context, not from Myles’s “Drafting Atelier” or another developer’s aesthetic.

## 8. Emerging brand principles — hypotheses, not final decisions

These are Discovery outputs to test in DEFINITION:

1. **Evidence over claims** — visual and verbal hierarchy should privilege what can be demonstrated.
2. **Systems beyond screens** — projects should feel connected to workflows, actors, data and operations.
3. **Editorial clarity** — fewer competing UI elements, stronger typography and intentional whitespace.
4. **Warm technical character** — preserve the existing stone/ink/copper direction unless Definition finds a stronger reason to change it.
5. **Quiet distinction** — one or two recognizable motifs are preferable to effects, gradients and animation everywhere.
6. **Progressive depth** — homepage for comprehension; case studies for architecture, QA and evidence.
7. **Human authorship** — the site should feel intentionally authored by Sebastián, not generated from a generic developer template.

## 9. Candidate positioning territory

Discovery does **not** select a tagline yet. It identifies a territory:

> Full-stack software builder who converts real operational problems into verifiable systems.

This preserves the recruiter-facing Full-Stack category while explaining why the portfolio contains hotel operations, rentals, logistics, applied AI, integrations and operational software.

The previous product brief’s proposition remains compatible:

> “Entiendo procesos operativos y los convierto en software confiable.”

Definition must determine whether this becomes public-facing copy, an internal brand principle, or is refined.

## 10. What should not be copied from the references

- No invented career counters, project counts or outcome metrics.
- No oversized archive simply to look prolific.
- No dark “developer cyber” aesthetic by default.
- No technology-logo wall as the main identity.
- No animation as proof of polish.
- No copying Myles’s plate/index/drafting vocabulary.
- No generic “passionate developer / crafting digital experiences” messaging.
